1. Starting point

The cushion is the lowest balance you're comfortable with. A common rule of thumb is one month of essential costs.

Try 14 extra days and leaving out planned work. If you still stay above your cushion, your cash flow is in good shape.

If a low week is coming

  • Invoice earlier. Send invoices the day work is approved, not at month end. Moving a planned invoice forward a week often fixes a dip on its own.
  • Shorten terms on new work. 14 days instead of 30, or a deposit up front, pulls money forward without changing your price.
  • Chase before it's late. A friendly note three days before the due date gets many invoices paid on time.
  • Move flexible costs. Annual software renewals, equipment and courses can usually wait a few weeks.
  • Know your slow payers. If one client is always 20 days late, put that into the forecast and your terms for them.

This is a planning aid, not financial advice. Forecasts are only as good as the dates you enter.
