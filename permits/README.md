# Permit lead feed: free sample (test, separate brand)

**What this is:** one week of new pool and pool-enclosure permits in Hillsborough County, FL, cleaned into a lead list for trades that sell to the homeowner at that address (screen enclosures, fencing, landscaping, pool service, outdoor lighting, patio furniture).

**Week:** Nov 2–8, 2026 (issued date). **Source:** Hillsborough County "Issued Permits" report from Accela, public ArcGIS item `3fd625c127734f0cafed1cfa648e1612`, updated daily (file read on Nov 15, 2026; newest issue date Nov 12). No login, no scraping.

A new pool permit is a lead for everyone who works around a new pool: **the enclosure, fence, landscaping, lighting and pool-service companies.** An enclosure permit tells the fence, landscaping and furniture trades the pool is nearly done.

## Files
- `sample-hillsborough-pool-permits-2026-11-02_08.csv`: the public sample. **No street address or permit number** in this public file: either one lets anyone look up the exact home on the county site. Public samples show only date, type, city, ZIP, value and work. The paid feed would carry full addresses, which are public record; it goes only to subscribers and never to a public repo.
- Columns: issued, type (New pool / Screen enclosure), city, zip, declared_value, work (cleaned description).

## Limits (honest)
- The county report has **no contractor or owner names**, and leaves out trade permits (electrical, plumbing) and permits still in review.
- County-issued permits only (HC-BLD numbers). Cities that run their own permitting, such as the **City of Tampa**, are separate feeds and not in this sample. Mailing city names like "Tampa 33618" can still be unincorporated county.
- Buyers should reach homeowners by **direct mail**. Florida's FTSA restricts unsolicited calls and texts to consumers.
