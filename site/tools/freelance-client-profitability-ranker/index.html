Which clients are worth keeping?

Rank your freelance clients by what they really pay per hour once calls, emails, revisions and invoice chasing are counted. See who to keep, who to re-price, and what letting one go would actually cost you. Everything runs in your browser and nothing is sent anywhere.

1. Your numbers

Target rate = what you'd quote a good new client today.

2. Your clients

For each client, enter what they paid you in the period and the hours you spent. Split the hours into the work itself and everything else (calls, emails, meetings, revisions beyond scope, chasing payments, waiting around). Estimates are fine.

3. Results

Add at least one client with money and hours to see results.

How the verdicts work

  • Effective rate = money received ÷ all hours (work + overhead).
  • Keep effective rate at least 90% of your target, payment usually within 2 weeks of the due date and stress below 4 out of 5.
  • Re-price or fix effective rate 60-89% of target, or one problem (slow payment, high overhead or high stress) on an otherwise decent client. The tool shows the rate that would bring them up to target at the same hours.
  • Consider letting go effective rate under 60% of target, or two or more problems at once.
  • Hours to replace = the client's income ÷ your target rate: how much work at your target rate would earn the same money.

This is a planning aid, not financial advice. A verdict is a starting point for a decision only you can make: a low-paying client may still be worth keeping for portfolio, learning or relationship reasons.
